## 2. The problem & the audience

- **Problem worth solving:** Groups want to book experiences together, but coordinating separate reservations, availability, preferences, and payments creates unnecessary friction. One organizer often carries the burden of coordinating everyone and may need to front the cost for the group.

- **Primary ICP:** Groups of **6–10 friends planning a destination trip or celebration**, with one organizer responsible for initiating and coordinating paid experiences and individual participants responsible for their own payments.

- **What they do today (the alternative):** Coordinate through messaging apps and shared documents, make separate bookings, manually confirm availability, and manage reimbursements through tools such as Venmo or Splitwise.

## 3. GTM motion

**Chosen motion: Hybrid — PLG Primary + CLG Secondary**

PLG fits Roamly's existing B2C model because travelers can discover, coordinate, book, and pay without sales involvement. CLG becomes the secondary growth layer because every group booking naturally requires the organizer to invite other travelers into the experience.

**PLG drives conversion. CLG amplifies acquisition.**

- **Acquisition:** Capture existing high-intent Roamly traffic and acquire additional users when organizers invite friends into group bookings.

- **Activation:** Organizer creates a group booking → invites friends → participants join → individual payments are completed.

- **Monetization:** Convert previously abandoned or fragmented group demand into completed, higher-value group bookings. Longer term, increase customer value as newly acquired group participants return to Roamly for future experiences.

## 6. The assumption I'm most worried about

**Assumption:** Travelers experiencing group coordination pain want Roamly to become part of the coordination workflow, rather than simply solving the transactional booking and payment problem.

If this assumption is wrong, features such as shared itinerary planning may add complexity without materially improving conversion.

**How I would test it:** Launch the minimum group flow centered on **one booking + shared availability + individual payments** and measure adoption and conversion. Use behavioral data, funnel analysis, and qualitative interviews with organizers and invited participants to determine whether additional planning and itinerary coordination features materially increase booking completion or customer value.

The first validation question is:

> **Do groups want Roamly to help them plan together, or primarily to let them book together and pay separately?**
